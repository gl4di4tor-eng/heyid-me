You have picked a place, you are sitting across from each other, and the first minute is a little awkward. That is completely normal. A few good questions can turn that stiff start into a real conversation.

This guide is for adults. It gives you forty questions that work on a first date, grouped by mood, plus a few habits that matter more than any question.

## The habits that matter most

1. **Listen more than you plan.** The question is only the door. What you do with the answer is the conversation.
2. **Follow up.** If someone mentions they love hiking, ask where. Curiosity is the best skill.
3. **Share your own answers.** A back-and-forth feels warm. An interview does not.
4. **Do not rush.** Silence of a few seconds is fine.
5. **Be honest.** You do not need to impress anyone.

## Warming up

1. How has your day been so far?
2. Have you been here before?
3. What was the best part of your week?
4. Are you a morning person or a night owl?
5. What did you do last weekend?
6. Do you have any plans coming up that you are looking forward to?
7. What is something small that made you smile today?
8. How did you find the place or the plan?

## Work and daily life

9. What do you do, and what do you like about it?
10. What did you want to be when you were a kid?
11. What is a typical day like for you?
12. If you could change one thing about your routine, what would it be?
13. What do you do to relax after a long day?
14. What is something you are learning right now?

## Interests and hobbies

15. What are you into lately?
16. What was the last show or film you really enjoyed?
17. What music have you been listening to?
18. Do you play any sport or do any activity regularly?
19. What is a hobby you would like to try?
20. What is the best book or story you have come across recently?

## Food and travel

21. What is your favorite food, and who cooks it best?
22. What is the best meal you have ever had?
23. Where is the best place you have traveled to?
24. Where would you go if you could travel tomorrow?
25. What is a place you have never been to but want to see?
26. What do you miss most when you are away from home?

## A little deeper

27. What is something you are proud of?
28. What makes you feel most like yourself?
29. What is a lesson that took you a long time to learn?
30. Who has had the biggest influence on you?
31. What does a perfect day look like for you?
32. What are you looking forward to in the next year?

## Playful

33. What is your most useless talent?
34. If you could have dinner with anyone, who would it be?
35. What is the strangest food you have ever tried?
36. Which fictional world would you like to visit?
37. What is your go-to karaoke song?
38. What would be the title of your autobiography?
39. Dogs, cats or neither?
40. What would you do with a free day and no obligations?

## What to avoid early on

Some topics are better left for later, because they can create pressure or tension before you have built any trust.

- **Exes in detail.** Mentioning that you have been through breakups is fine. Replaying them is not.
- **Money and salary.** See [whether it is rude to ask about age, salary or religion](/blog/is-it-rude-to-ask-about-age-salary-or-religion/).
- **Heavy politics or religion** as a debate.
- **Complaining for a long time.** A little is human. A lot is draining.
- **Rapid-fire questions.** Mix in stories of your own.

## When the conversation stalls

It happens to everyone. Here is what helps.

- **Go back to something they said earlier.** "You mentioned hiking, tell me about your best trip."
- **Notice your surroundings.** "This place has a great view, have you been to the area before?"
- **Suggest an activity.** A short walk can reset the mood.
- **Say it out loud.** "I got a bit quiet, I'm enjoying this" is honest and friendly.

## If your date speaks another language

If you are meeting someone from another country, the same questions work, but keep your sentences simple and avoid idioms. Our list of [50 questions to ask someone from another country](/blog/50-questions-to-ask-someone-from-another-country/) is a good companion, and [small talk around the world](/blog/small-talk-around-the-world/) explains how topics differ between cultures.

## Choosing the place

The setting shapes the conversation. A quiet café is easier than a loud bar. Our guide to [where to go on a first date](/blog/where-to-go-on-a-first-date-25-ideas-for-any-budget/) has twenty-five ideas.

## How to end the date well

You do not have to decide everything on the spot. If you had a good time, say so: "I really enjoyed this, I'd like to do it again." If you are not sure, a warm "Thank you, it was nice meeting you" is kind and clear. If it was not a match, you can be polite without making promises you will not keep. Our guide to [ending a chat politely](/blog/how-to-end-a-chat-politely-without-ghosting/) has wording you can borrow.

## The short version

Good first date conversation comes from curiosity, listening and honesty, not from perfect questions. Pick a few from this list, ask them as if you want to know the answer, and let the conversation go where it wants.

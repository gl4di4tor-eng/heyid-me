"Hey." "Hi." "What's up?" If those are the first messages you usually send, you are not alone, and you have probably noticed that they rarely lead anywhere. The person on the other side has nothing to react to, so they reply with one word or nothing at all.

A good first message is not clever. It is specific, friendly and easy to answer. This guide gives you thirty examples for different situations, and the rules behind them so you can write your own.

## The rules of a good first message

1. **Be specific.** Mention something real: a shared interest, a detail, a place.
2. **Ask one easy question.** Not three, and nothing too personal.
3. **Keep it short.** Two or three lines is enough.
4. **Be warm, not clever.** Friendly beats witty.
5. **Do not copy and paste.** People can tell.
6. **Skip comments on appearance.** They rarely land well, and they say nothing about you as a person.

## When you share an interest

1. "I saw you are into photography. What is the best place you have shot?"
2. "I noticed you like climbing too. Do you have a favorite spot?"
3. "You mentioned that show. I loved the first season. Did you like how it ended?"
4. "I'm trying to get into cooking. What is the one dish you are best at?"
5. "I see you play that game. Which character do you play?"
6. "Fellow book person here. What is the best thing you have read this year?"
7. "I'm learning guitar. How long did it take you to get decent?"
8. "I love that band too. Have you seen them live?"
9. "Your running route sounds great. Is it flat or hilly?"
10. "You like hiking, right? Any trail you would recommend for a beginner?"

## When you have only a little to go on

11. "Hi! How is your day going so far?"
12. "Hello from (your city)! Where are you chatting from?"
13. "What are you up to this evening?"
14. "Morning! Are you having coffee or tea?"
15. "I'm taking a break and decided to say hi. What is your day like?"
16. "I like your username. Is there a story behind it?"
17. "I'm new here. Is there anything you would recommend?"
18. "Hi there! What brings you to this app?"

## When the other person speaks another language

19. "Hello! I'm learning your language. Could you teach me one word today?"
20. "Hi! What is a food from your country I should try?"
21. "I would love to hear what a normal weekend looks like where you live."
22. "What is the weather like in your city right now?"
23. "How do you say 'hello' in your language?"
24. "What is a song from your country that you love?"

If you are chatting through a translation tool, keep your sentences short. See [chat translation](/blog/chat-translation-how-to-talk-to-anyone-in-their-language/) for tips.

## When you want something playful

25. "Quick question: pineapple on pizza, yes or no?"
26. "If you could eat any meal today, what would it be?"
27. "Important matter: coffee or tea?"
28. "What is the best snack for a long day?"
29. "Be honest: are you a morning person or a night owl?"
30. "What is a small thing that always makes you happy?"

## What to do after they reply

The first message is the door. The second is where the conversation starts.

- **Answer their reply properly.** Show that you read it.
- **Follow up on one detail.** "You said you hiked there, was it crowded?"
- **Share something about yourself.** A short answer to your own question keeps things balanced.
- **Be patient with timing.** People reply at different speeds. See [why someone stopped replying](/blog/why-someone-stopped-replying-it-may-not-be-you/).

## What not to send

- **Just "hi" with nothing else.** It is easy to ignore.
- **Long messages about yourself.** Let them breathe.
- **Anything that sounds like a copied line.**
- **Demands.** "Send me a photo" or "reply now" are red flags.
- **Requests for money or personal data.** Never. And if someone sends you one, read [scam red flags when chatting with someone abroad](/blog/scam-red-flags-when-chatting-with-someone-abroad/).

## Flat openers and how to fix them

| Flat opener | Better version |
|---|---|
| hey | Hey! I saw you like climbing. Where do you usually go? |
| sup? | Hi! How is your week going so far? |
| Wanna chat? | Hi, I'm curious about people. What is the most interesting thing you did today? |
| You look nice | I like your profile photo. Where was it taken? |

The better versions mention something real and end with a question that is easy to answer.

## Adjusting your message to the place

On a hobby forum, a message about the shared interest works best. On a random chat app, where nobody has a profile, short and friendly is best. On a language exchange site, mention what you would like to practice. The more the message fits the place, the easier it is to answer.

## Overcoming the fear

Many people never send a first message because they are afraid of being ignored. Remember that a lack of reply is rarely about you. People are busy, shy or simply not looking to chat. Sending a friendly message costs you nothing, and when it works, it can lead to something good. For more on building confidence, read [how to meet new people online without feeling awkward](/blog/how-to-meet-new-people-online-without-feeling-awkward/).

## The short version

Be specific, warm and brief. Ask one easy question. Use these thirty messages as they are, or change a few words so they sound like you. And then do the most important thing, which is to send one.

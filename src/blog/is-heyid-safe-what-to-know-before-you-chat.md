If you are about to talk to strangers through an app, asking "is it safe?" is the right first question. This page tries to answer it honestly, and that means saying what we know, what we do not, and what is up to you.

A quick note on who is writing. I'm Paweł, the developer of HEYID, so I am not a neutral reviewer. Treat this as a clear explanation from the person who built it, not as a guarantee.

## What HEYID is

HEYID is a random chat app. You tap, the app matches you with another user, and you start talking, either by text, voice messages, photos or stickers. It translates what you both write, and live voice translation is part of the Premium plan. The app is free to start and does not make you fill in a long profile first.

That last point matters for safety, because the less personal information you put online, the less there is to misuse. It also means you cannot check who is on the other side, and that is true for every random chat app.

## What you can do to stay in control

According to the app's description, you can block, report or end any conversation instantly. Use those tools early. If something feels off, you do not need a reason and you do not owe anyone an explanation. Blocking takes one tap, and reporting helps protect the next person who would have met the same account.

The app also includes an AI helper called SPARX, which suggests messages when you are not sure what to say. It helps with starting conversations, not with judging who is trustworthy.

## What the store listing says about data

The Google Play listing includes a Data safety section, written by the developer. At the time of writing, it says the following:

- No data is shared with third parties.
- The app may collect personal info, messages and other data types.
- You can request that your data be deleted.
- The listing also notes that data isn't encrypted.

You can read the full details on the store page and in the [app's privacy policy](https://gl4di4tor-eng.github.io/heyid-privacy-policy). The practical lesson is the same for any chat app: treat your conversations as not fully private. Do not send passwords, bank details, ID documents or anything you would not want to see repeated.

## Who it is for

The store lists a 12+ rating with a parental guidance recommendation, and it labels the app as one where users interact with each other. In plain words, you will be talking to real people you do not know. Chats with strangers are better suited to adults. If you are a teenager, talk to a parent or another trusted adult before you use any random chat app, and never share your school, address or photos you would not want made public.

## What no random chat app can promise

Be skeptical of any app that says it is perfectly safe. A random chat connects you with people who have all kinds of intentions. Most are curious and friendly. Some are bored, rude or looking to scam. The tools above help you react quickly, but they cannot decide who you meet.

## A practical safety checklist

1. **Keep personal details private.** No full name, address, workplace, school or phone number.
2. **Do not send money.** Whatever the reason, and however long you have been talking.
3. **Be careful with links and files.** If someone pushes you to click something or install something, stop.
4. **Do not move to another app too quickly.** Moving away from the chat removes your ability to report.
5. **Trust your instincts.** If a conversation makes you uncomfortable, end it.
6. **Use block and report.** Do not wait until you are sure.

HEYID gives you tools to stay in control: blocking, reporting and ending a chat at any time. The store listing is open about what data may be collected, and about the fact that it isn't encrypted.

You arrived, you unpacked, and now the apartment is quiet. You do not know anyone, you are not sure where to go, and your phone is full of messages from friends far away. Moving to a new city or country is exciting, and it can also be lonely.

That feeling is common, and it passes. Here is a practical plan to meet people, find your community and feel at home.

## Give yourself time, and expect the dip

Almost everyone who moves feels a dip after the first few weeks. The excitement fades and the daily reality sets in. It does not mean you made a mistake. Friendships take time, and you are starting from zero. Treat the first few months as an investment, and be kind to yourself.

## A simple plan for the first weeks

**Week one: get your bearings.** Learn your neighborhood. Find a regular café, a grocery store and a park. Familiar faces start to appear when you go to the same places.

**Weeks two to four: join something regular.** Pick two or three activities that repeat weekly. That is the secret: the same people, again and again.

**Month two and beyond: deepen.** Invite someone for coffee, suggest a weekend plan, say yes to invitations.

## Where to meet people

1. **Language classes.** You meet other newcomers and locals who want to practice.
2. **Language exchange events.** Many cities have regular meetups where people practice languages over a drink.
3. **Sports and fitness.** Running clubs, climbing gyms, yoga classes and team sports.
4. **Volunteering.** A great way to meet locals and feel useful.
5. **Co-working spaces and community centers.**
6. **Expat and newcomer groups.** People in the same situation understand how you feel.
7. **Hobby clubs.** Photography, board games, hiking, music.
8. **Work or study.** Colleagues and classmates are a natural start.
9. **Your neighbors.** A friendly hello can lead to a lot.

## Use online tools to find offline people

Apps and websites can introduce you before you arrive. Search for local groups, events and communities in your new city and join a few in advance. You can also chat online with people who live there. ## Learn a little of the local language

You do not need to be fluent to be liked. A few phrases such as hello, thank you and "do you speak English?" show respect and make people friendlier. If the language is new to you, [how to chat confidently in a language you're learning](/blog/how-to-chat-confidently-in-a-language-youre-learning/) helps with the fear of making mistakes. ## Understand the local social habits

Every place has its own rules for friendliness. In some places, people chat easily with strangers. In others, they are more reserved at first and warm up slowly. Neither is a sign that you are unwelcome. Our guides on [small talk around the world](/blog/small-talk-around-the-world/) and [how to be polite in different cultures](/blog/how-to-be-polite-in-different-cultures/) can help you read the situation.

## A sample week

Here is what a realistic week might look like in your second month: Monday evening, a language class. Wednesday morning, a regular coffee at the same café. Friday, a volunteer shift or a sports session. Saturday, a local event you found online. Sunday, a call home. That is only four or five commitments, and by the end of the month you will recognize faces, which is the first step toward friendship.

## Say yes, and invite

In the beginning, say yes to almost everything that is safe and comfortable. An invitation to a birthday, a hike or a dinner is a chance. Then become the person who invites. Organizing something simple, such as a coffee, a walk or a game night, makes you more visible and gives others permission to follow.

## Stay connected to home, but not only to home

Calls and messages with people back home are comforting, and you should keep them. But if every spare hour goes to the old life, it is harder to build a new one. Try to balance the two.

## Be safe while you meet new people

New places mean new risks. A few habits help.

- Meet new people in public places first.
- Tell a friend where you are going.
- Be careful with strangers who ask for money or personal information. Our guide to [scam red flags when chatting with someone abroad](/blog/scam-red-flags-when-chatting-with-someone-abroad/) lists the warning signs.
- Trust your instincts.

## When it feels like too much

If the loneliness is heavy, or lasts for months, talk to someone. A friend, a family member, a doctor or a counselor can help. Many cities and universities have support services for newcomers, and asking for help is a normal part of settling in.

## The short version

Meeting people in a new place takes time, repetition and a few small risks. Join regular activities, learn a few local phrases, say yes to invitations, invite others and be patient with yourself. Community does not appear overnight, but it does appear.

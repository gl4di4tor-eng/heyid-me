Everyone runs out of things to say sometimes, even with close friends. With someone from another country the pauses can feel longer, because you are also dealing with a language gap and different habits. The trick is not to fill every silence at any cost. It is to have a few reliable tools ready. Here are the ones that work best.

## A pause is not a failure

The first thing to know is that a short pause is normal. In text chat, the other person may be typing slowly in a second language, translating a word, or simply making a cup of tea. Before you panic and send three messages in a row, wait a minute or two.

Some cultures are also far more comfortable with silence than others. In many Nordic countries and in Japan, a quiet moment in conversation is often seen as thoughtful rather than awkward. If you treat silence as a problem, you may end up rushing someone who was perfectly happy.

## Ask follow-up questions, not new questions

The most common mistake is jumping to a brand new question every time. It feels like an interview. A follow-up question, on the other hand, grows out of what the other person just said, and it shows you were listening.

Suppose someone tells you they spent the weekend hiking. You could ask "What is your favourite food?" which comes out of nowhere. Or you could ask "Where did you go? Was the weather good?" The second version builds on their own words, so it feels natural and it is easy for them to answer. A few reliable follow-ups are "What was that like?", "How did you get into that?" and "What is the best part about it?"

## Share a small story

A chat where one person only asks and the other only answers gets tiring quickly. Give something back. When they mention hiking, tell them about the worst walk you ever did, or the one place you would love to visit. Stories do not need to be impressive. A two-sentence memory with one small detail is perfect.

Stories also give the other person something to react to, and they often trigger a story in return. That back-and-forth is what turns a list of answers into a real conversation.

## Use callbacks

A callback is a return to something that came up earlier. "You said you were learning guitar. How is that going?" Callbacks are powerful because they show that you remember, and almost everyone enjoys being remembered. They also give you a free source of topics, since nearly every chat contains at least three or four details worth coming back to.

If you tend to forget, keep a tiny note of two or three things people tell you. It is not manipulative. It is the same thing a good friend does by paying attention.

## Bridge to a new topic gracefully

Sometimes a topic really is finished, and the best move is to change it. Do it with a bridge instead of a jump. "Speaking of food, have you ever tried something you thought you would hate?" connects the new topic to the old one with a single phrase. Useful bridges include "that reminds me", "speaking of", "on a different note" and "by the way".

If you simply have nothing, it is fine to say so. "I am blanking on what to ask, so tell me something good that happened this week." Honesty is charming, and the question is open enough to carry the chat for several more minutes.

## When you are completely stuck

Keep a few universal questions in your pocket. What is a food from your country that everyone should try? What is the funniest word in your language? What is a small thing that always makes your day better? These work with nearly anyone because they invite a personal answer without being too private.

## Know when to end the conversation

Part of keeping a conversation going is knowing that not every chat needs to last forever. A good ending is as valuable as a good beginning. If the energy is fading, say something kind and specific: "It was great talking about your hiking trip. I need to get going, but I would love to hear how the next one goes." That leaves a door open without any pressure.

It also helps the other person feel good about the chat, which makes them more likely to come back. And if the conversation is making you uncomfortable, you can end it at any moment without explaining yourself. Good chat apps let you block or report someone, and you should use that freedom when you need it.

## A quick recap

Wait before you worry. Follow up on what they said. Give a small story back. Return to earlier details. Bridge to new topics. Be honest when you are stuck.

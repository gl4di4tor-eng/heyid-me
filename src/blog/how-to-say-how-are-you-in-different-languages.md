In English, "How are you?" is rarely a real question. Most of the time it means "hello," and the expected answer is "Good, you?" Nobody wants a medical report.

Other languages treat it differently. Some use it as a greeting too. Some expect a real answer. Some barely use it at all. Here are twenty ways to ask, with rough pronunciations, and a few notes on how each one is used.

The pronunciations are approximations for English speakers. Treat them as a starting point, and let a native speaker correct you.

## When it is a greeting, and when it is a question

**As a greeting.** In Brazilian Portuguese, [[Tudo bem?]] works as both hello and question, and the answer is usually [[Tudo bem, e você?]] ("All good, and you?"). French [[Ça va ?]] works the same way: the reply is [[Ça va, et toi ?]] You do not need to share your life story.

**As a real question.** In German, Polish and Russian, people often take the question more literally than English speakers do, so an honest, short answer is normal. If you are having a rough day, you are allowed to say so. That varies by person, though, and many people still reply with a quick "fine."

**With a literal meaning.** The Hebrew [[מה נשמע?]] means "what is heard?" and the Swahili [[Habari?]] means "news?" The usual Swahili answer is [[Nzuri]] ("good"). In both cases, the phrase is really asking what is new.

## Notes on specific languages

**Japanese.** [[お元気ですか？]] is not a casual hello. People use it when they have not seen someone for a while, so it can feel oddly formal if you ask it every day. With friends, a simple [[元気？]] ("genki?") is more natural.

**Mandarin.** [[你好吗？]] is the version every textbook teaches. In everyday chat, many people ask something more like [[最近怎么样？]] (zuìjìn zěnmeyàng), "how have things been lately?" Among older people, you may also hear [[吃了吗？]] ("have you eaten?"), which started as a way to show care.

**Arabic.** The phrase changes with the gender of the person you ask: [[كيف حالك]] to a man and [[كيف حالكِ]] to a woman. A common answer is [[الحمد لله]] ("praise be to God"), pronounced al-HAM-du lil-LAH.

**Korean.** [[잘 지내세요?]] is polite. Among friends, you would say [[잘 지내?]] and drop the ending. Korean has several levels of speech, so it is worth asking how someone prefers to be addressed.

## How to answer

Learning to ask is half the job. Here are easy replies, each one followed by the polite "and you?"

| Language | Reply | Say it like |
|---|---|---|
| Spanish | [[Bien, gracias. ¿Y tú?]] | byen GRAH-syahs ee TOO |
| French | [[Ça va bien, merci. Et toi ?]] | sah vah byan mehr-SEE eh TWAH |
| German | [[Gut, danke. Und dir?]] | goot DAHN-kuh oont DEER |
| Italian | [[Bene, grazie. E tu?]] | BEH-neh GRAHT-see-eh eh TOO |
| Portuguese | [[Tudo bem, e você?]] | TOO-doo behng ee voh-SEH |
| Japanese | [[元気です。あなたは？]] | GEN-kee dess. ah-nah-tah wah |

Always ask the question back. In almost every culture it is a sign of good manners, and it keeps the chat moving.

A few small mistakes to avoid: using the formal form with a close friend, which can sound cold, using the informal form with an older person, which can sound careless, and answering with a long story when the other person only wanted a quick greeting.

## Formal or informal?

Many of these phrases have formal and informal versions. Spanish [[¿Cómo estás?]] becomes [[¿Cómo está?]] with someone you should address formally. In Hindi, [[आप कैसे हैं?]] is the respectful form, and [[तुम कैसे हो?]] is the casual one. With someone you have just met, the polite form is the safer choice, until they tell you otherwise. Our guide to [formal and informal you](/blog/formal-and-informal-you-in-different-languages/) explains how to choose.

## A simple way to use this

1. Start with hello in their language, from our [20 ways to say hello](/blog/20-ways-to-say-hello-in-different-languages/).
2. Add "how are you" from this list.
3. Wait for the answer, then reply to it in a friendly way.
4. Finish with a thank you, which you can find in [how to say thank you in 20 languages](/blog/how-to-say-thank-you-in-different-languages/).

You can also try the [say hello tool](/discover/say-hello/) for a random greeting with audio.

## Last thought

You do not have to say any of this perfectly. A slightly clumsy [[¿Cómo estás?]] from a stranger is a small, friendly gift. Pick one language, practice one phrase and use it on someone real this week.

For about fifteen years, Omegle was one of the most famous places on the internet to talk to a stranger. You pressed a button and landed in a text or video chat with someone you had never met. Then, in November 2023, it was gone.

People still search for it every day, usually with one question: what can I use instead? This guide answers it, but with a warning. Not every "alternative" is a good idea, and the reason Omegle closed is something worth understanding before you pick a replacement.

A note before we start: we make HEYID, a random chat app, so we are not neutral. We mention it as one example and you should judge it with the same questions we suggest for others.

## What happened to Omegle

Omegle shut down on November 8, 2023. Its founder, Leif K-Brooks, wrote a farewell message saying that running the site was no longer sustainable, financially or psychologically. He pointed to years of misuse of the platform and to the legal and financial pressure of fighting it. The site closed after a lawsuit and a wider storm of concern about how it was being used.

The lesson is not that talking to strangers online is always wrong. It is that a service that puts strangers together, with no rules, no age checks and weak moderation, tends to attract the worst behavior. Keep that in mind when you look at the alternatives.

## Beware of fake Omegle sites

When a famous site closes, copycats appear. Many "Omegle" clones use similar names and layouts, and some are designed to collect data, push ads or spread malware. A few warning signs:

- The site asks you to download a program or browser extension to "continue."
- It asks for payment to "verify" your age or identity.
- It has no information about who runs it.
- It pushes you to click ads or links.

If a site looks like that, close it.

## What to look for in an alternative

Instead of asking "which service is best," ask "does this service protect me?" Here is a checklist.

1. **Age rules.** Does it say who it is for, and does it try to keep adults and minors apart?
2. **Blocking and reporting.** Can you end a chat, block someone and report them in a couple of taps?
3. **Moderation.** Does the service say how it responds to reports?
4. **No forced video.** Text-first services let you stay in control of what you show.
5. **Data and privacy.** Does it explain what it collects, and can you ask for deletion?
6. **A real contact.** Is there an email or support page with a real person behind it?
7. **No pressure to pay up front.** A free trial is normal. Being told to pay for "safety" is not.

## The kinds of alternatives that exist

**Random chat apps.** Apps like HEYID pair you with strangers, usually in text or voice. Some add features such as translation and block and report tools. Look at the store listing and the privacy policy before you use any of them. We explain our own in [is HEYID safe](/blog/is-heyid-safe-what-to-know-before-you-chat/).

**Video roulette sites.** Chatroulette, which launched in 2009, and Emerald Chat are often named in lists of alternatives. Their rules and moderation change over time, so check their current policies yourself before you use them. Video chat with strangers carries more risk than text.

**Language exchange communities.** If you want to practice a language, communities built around exchange are usually friendlier and more focused.

**Interest-based communities.** Forums, Discord servers and groups centered on a hobby bring together people who already share something with you.

## Safer habits, wherever you chat

1. **Keep personal information private.** No full name, address, school or workplace.
2. **Never send money.** Read [scam red flags when chatting with someone abroad](/blog/scam-red-flags-when-chatting-with-someone-abroad/).
3. **Think before you turn on a camera.** Anything on video can be recorded.
4. **Do not click unknown links.**
5. **Use block and report.** Do it early.
6. **Stay on the platform** so that you can report problems.
7. **Take breaks.** If a chat makes you feel uneasy, stop.

## If you are under 18

Most random chat services are designed for adults, and many have age rules for good reason. If you are a teenager, a random chat with strangers is not the best place to spend time. Talk to a parent or another trusted adult. Look for moderated communities built around a hobby or a school activity instead.

## Why the demand has not gone away

People like talking to strangers because it is surprising, because it can feel low-pressure and because sometimes it is exactly what you need when you feel bored or alone. The best alternatives keep that spark while adding what Omegle lacked: rules, tools and a real attempt to protect users. ## The short version

Omegle closed in 2023 after years of misuse. If you look for something similar, avoid copycat sites, and judge any service by its age rules, blocking tools, moderation and privacy. Keep your details private, never send money and leave any chat that feels wrong.

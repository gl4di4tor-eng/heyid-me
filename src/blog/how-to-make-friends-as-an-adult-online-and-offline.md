At school, friends came built in. You saw the same people every day, and friendship simply happened. As an adult, that structure disappears. Work takes your time, people move, schedules fill up, and suddenly you realize you are not sure how to make a new friend.

If that is you, you are in very good company. Many adults say they find it harder to make friends than they did when they were younger, and it is not a sign that something is wrong with you. It just needs a more deliberate approach.

## Why it feels harder

- **Less automatic contact.** You do not share a classroom or a dorm anymore.
- **Busy lives.** Everyone has work, family and obligations.
- **More caution.** Adults worry about being rejected or being a burden.
- **Friends move.** People change cities and circumstances.

None of these is permanent, and all of them can be worked with.

## Friendship needs repeated time together

Researchers who study friendship say that it takes a lot of time together to turn an acquaintance into a friend. One often-cited study, by communication scholar Jeffrey Hall, suggested that it takes roughly fifty hours to move from acquaintance to casual friend, and many more for a close one. The exact numbers are less important than the idea: friendship comes from repeated contact, not from a single great conversation.

That is why the best place to make friends is somewhere you will see the same people again and again.

## Where to find people offline

1. **Regular classes.** Language, dance, art or cooking. Weekly repetition is perfect.
2. **Sports and fitness groups.** Running clubs, climbing gyms and team sports.
3. **Volunteering.** You meet people who care about the same things.
4. **Book clubs and hobby groups.**
5. **Co-working spaces and community centers.**
6. **Your neighborhood.** Say hello to neighbors and regulars at your local café.
7. **Work.** Colleagues can become friends, if you want them to.

## Where to find people online

1. **Interest communities.** Forums, groups and servers centered on something you love.
2. **Language exchange.** People who want to practice are usually friendly and open.
3. **Online classes and workshops.** You meet people who share a goal.
4. **Gaming communities.** Playing together gives a shared activity.
5. **Chat apps.** Random chat can lead to real connections if you use it carefully. See [how to meet new people online without feeling awkward](/blog/how-to-meet-new-people-online-without-feeling-awkward/).

## Turning an acquaintance into a friend

This is the step where many people get stuck. A few habits help.

- **Take the first step.** Most people are waiting for someone else to suggest something.
- **Suggest something small.** "Want to grab a coffee after class?" is easy to say yes to.
- **Follow up.** A message the next day, such as "That was fun, let's do it again," builds momentum.
- **Remember details.** Ask about the thing they mentioned last time.
- **Be reliable.** Show up when you say you will.
- **Give it time.** Friendship rarely forms in a week.

## Messages you can send

Sometimes the hardest part is the wording. Here are three easy ones.

- "I enjoyed talking after class. Would you like to grab a coffee sometime?"
- "That was fun! I'm heading to the same event next week, are you going?"
- "I just found a great place for lunch near work. Want to try it together?"

Short, friendly and easy to say yes to. If the answer is no or silence, that is fine. Try someone else.

## Dealing with rejection

Not every invitation will lead to a yes. People are busy, cautious or simply not interested. That is not a verdict on you. A useful way to think about it is that you are not looking for everyone, you are looking for a few people who click. Try more than one thing, and expect that some will not work.

## Online friendships are real

Some people feel that friendships formed online do not count. They do. What matters is whether you feel supported, understood and glad to talk. Many people have close friends they met online and have never met in person. The same rules apply: be kind, be consistent and look out for scams. Keep your personal information private at first, and never send money.

## Loneliness and what to do about it

If you feel lonely, you are not failing. It is a very human feeling, and it is common. Try to build a few small connections rather than waiting for one perfect friend. If the feeling is heavy or lasts for a long time, talking to someone you trust or a professional can help.

## The short version

Making friends as an adult takes repeated contact, small invitations and a bit of courage. Go where the same people appear regularly, take the first step, follow up and be patient. One good friendship is worth a lot of effort, and it often begins with a single message.
